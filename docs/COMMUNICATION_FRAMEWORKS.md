## Website application

MATCH guides discovery, reflection, clarification, and relevant offer
connections. Reflect only answers the visitor actually supplied. Clarify
through the existing intake. Present optional offer facts without claiming
that a fit has already been established. This introduces no automated
recommendations.

CRIB addresses hesitation by acknowledging the visitor's purpose and offering
compatible next steps. For example, a visitor who asks about an earlier failed
attempt wants the next attempt to be worthwhile. Invite the context and what
was learned, then discuss a manageable piece of work.

The practical distinction here is discovery and understanding versus working
through hesitation toward a compatible next step. These application notes do
not replace the missing original definitions and examples.

Use everyday language, recognize difficulties without assuming distress, and
move toward possibilities and concrete help. Promise delivery only within
agreed work. Keep changing implementation and validation evidence in the
existing factual ledgers.
